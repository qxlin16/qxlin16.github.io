About Me

Hello! I am Qixuan, a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Washington, working in Prof. Mo Li’s lab. My research focuses on developing a novel solid-state beam steering technology – acousto-optic beam steering (AOBS) – to overcome long-standing challenges in optical beam steering and control that underpin many advanced optical technologies.

AOBS leverages Brillouin scattering, where traveling acoustic waves create dynamic gratings that diffract light from a photonic chip into free space. This technology unlocks unique capibilities, such as frequency-angular resolving (FAR) LiDAR, where a beam’s steering angle is intrinsically labeled in its optical frequency, enabling 3D imaging in the frequency domain. Additionally, multi-tone acoustic waves can co-propagate within a single beam steering channel. By integrating an array of such channels, the system can readily scale to simultaneous steer hundreds of individully controlable beams from a compact chip.

Our work has been published in Nature and Nature Communications, with exciting future directions focused on both fundamental studies of material and acoustic properties, and the engineering of optomechanical circuits to boost efficiency. These developments aim to unlock new functionalities and enable seamless integration with other nonlinear optical effects, pushing the frontiers of what this technology can achieve — stay tuned!
