Hello! I am Qixuan, a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Washington, working in Prof. Mo Li’s lab. My research focuses on developing a novel solid-state beam steering technology – acousto-optic beam steering (AOBS) – to address long-standing challenges in optical beam steering and control that underpin many advanced optical technologies.

AOBS is based on Brillouin scattering, where traveling acoustic waves form dynamic gratings that scatter light from a photonic chip into free space. This technology enables novel capibilities, such as frequency-angular resolving (FAR) LiDAR, where a beam’s steering angle is intrinsically encoded in its optical frequency, allowing 3D imaging in the frequency domain. Additionally, multi-tone acoustic waves can co-propagate within a single beam steering channel. By integrating an array of such channels, the system can readily scale to simultaneous steer hundreds of individully controlable beams from a compact chip.

Our work has been published in Nature and accepted by Nature Communications, with more exciting developments coming soon – stay tuned!

We are also exploring the co-integration of acousto-optic devices with other nonlinear optical effects – such as electro-optic and Kerr frequency comb – to unlock new possibilities and expand application frontiers.
